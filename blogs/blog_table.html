What you observe More likely depression More likely dementia
Memory changes Complaints about memory loss; often says "I can't remember anything"; awareness of the problem is intact Unaware of memory failures; denies problems or confabulates (fills in gaps with invented content)
Onset Family can often identify when the change began It's difficult to pinpoint when it started
Mood Consistently low, hopeless, or irritable; little variation across the day Variable; may have periods of normal mood; sundowning (worse in evenings) is common
Response to questions "I don't know" and "I don't care" disengaged, but answers are often accurate when pressed Wrong answers delivered with confidence; repetition of questions they have already asked
Response to treatment Significant improvement possible with appropriate treatment — often within weeks Decline is progressive; treatment manages symptoms but does not reverse progression
Physical symptoms Prominent — headaches, fatigue, pain, and appetite change are often the presenting complaints Physical symptoms appear later in the disease course; early stages are primarily cognitive
